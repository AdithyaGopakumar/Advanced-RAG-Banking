# Demand Draft

## Overview

A Demand Draft (DD) is a pre-paid, negotiable payment instrument issued by the bank on behalf of a customer. Because the bank debits the customer's account upfront at the time of issuance, a DD guarantees payment and cannot "bounce" due to insufficient funds. It is heavily used in educational fee payments, government tenders, and real estate transactions.

---

## How It Works

1. **Issuance:** The customer requests a DD by filling out a physical form at the branch or requesting it via NetBanking.
2. **Funding:** The bank debits the requested amount plus DD issuance charges from the customer's account.
3. **Printing:** The bank prints a physical draft payable to the specific beneficiary.
4. **Delivery:** The customer physically hands over or mails the DD to the beneficiary.
5. **Clearing:** The beneficiary deposits the DD in their bank account, and it clears through the standard CTS (Cheque Truncation System) like a regular cheque.

---

## Validity and Revalidation

- **Validity:** A Demand Draft is valid for **3 months** from the date of issue.
- **Stale DD:** If not presented within 3 months, it becomes stale. The beneficiary cannot cash it.
- **Revalidation:** The original purchaser can request the issuing branch to revalidate a stale DD for another 3 months by surrendering the original instrument. Revalidation charges apply.

---

## Cancellation and Lost Drafts

- **Cancellation:** If the customer no longer needs the DD, they can cancel it and have the funds credited back to their account. *Requirement:* The customer must surrender the original, physical DD to the issuing branch. Cancellation charges apply.
- **Lost DD:** If the physical draft is lost or stolen, the purchaser must submit an indemnity bond and a police complaint (FIR) to the issuing branch to get a duplicate DD issued or to cancel the lost draft.

---

## Charges

- DD issuance attracts a tiered fee based on the draft amount.
- Additional charges apply for Cancellation, Revalidation, or issuing a Duplicate DD.
- See [Payment Charges](../charges/payment-charges.md) for exact fee slabs.
